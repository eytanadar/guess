1 Why networks?

Fraud, money laundering and organised crime are hard to see in a table. One claim, one transfer or one company looks normal on its own. The pattern only appears in the connections:

  • the same phone number on six “unrelated” claims;
  • a ring of accounts that passes money around and back to where it started;
  • a dozen companies with one director;
  • an account that collects many small deposits and sends a few large ones.

Investigative network analysis is mostly three moves:

  1. Resolve duplicate entities, so one person is one node.
  2. Link entities through what they share.
  3. Score the groups and patterns that result, so a person can review the few that matter.

This book assumes the Kitchen. Its data are simulated. Real investigation data are confidential, and simulated data let the recipes prove they find what was planted. The people, companies and accounts are invented.

2 Getting oriented

2.1 Look at a multi-type network Basic

You have a network whose nodes are different kinds of thing. You want to see what connects to what.

makeFromGDF("claims-SIMULATED.gdf")
colorize(kind)
shapeBy(kind)
legendFor(kind)
[(grp[0].kind, len(grp)) for grp in groupBy(kind)]

How it works. Investigation data almost always mix entity types: people and the identifiers they use (phones, addresses, emails, cars, bank accounts), and the events that tie them together (claims, transactions, calls). Colour and shape by type first, and count each kind, before any analysis. Most structure, and most mistakes, come from how the types connect.

3 Resolving entities

3.1 Find duplicate people Intermediate

You want to find people entered more than once: typos, nicknames, reordered names, different formatting.

Ingredients: claims-SIMULATED.gdf · findDuplicates · mergeNodes

makeFromGDF("claims-SIMULATED.gdf")
people = (kind == "person")
pairs = findDuplicates(label, among=people, threshold=0.7, show=False)
len(pairs)
def phones(p): return set(x for x in p.getNeighbors() if x.kind == "phone")
confirmed = [(a, b, sc) for a, b, sc in pairs if phones(a) & phones(b)]
len(confirmed)
mergeNodes(confirmed)

How it works. findDuplicates(field) compares names after normalising them: case, accents and punctuation removed, words sorted (so “Pell Shawn” matches “Shawn Pell”), and company suffixes and titles (Ltd, Inc, Mr) dropped. It scores each pair from 0 to 1 and returns the pairs above threshold for review. It never merges anything itself. Here it finds 2,405 pairs at 0.7. Most are namesakes, different people with similar names, which is exactly what happens in real data. Requiring a second piece of evidence (a shared phone) leaves 25 pairs, which are exactly the 25 planted duplicates.

mergeNodes(pairs) then merges each pair. The second node’s edges move to the first (parallel edges combine their weights), the first node’s empty fields are filled from the second, aliases records the other names, and merged counts how many records the node now stands for. It’s one undo step.

Pitfalls.

  • Never merge on a name alone. A false merge invents a connection that no one made, and in an investigation that’s an accusation. Merge on agreement of independent identifiers (phone, date of birth, address), keep the aliases, and keep the undo.
  • Lower the threshold to find more, then filter. At 0.85 the name match finds only 17 of the 25 duplicates, because initials and reordered names score lower. Find broadly, then confirm strictly.

4 Shared identifiers

4.1 Link people by what they share Intermediate

You want groups of people tied together by shared phones and addresses.

makeFromGDF("claims-SIMULATED.gdf")
project(kind, "person")
removeDisconnected()
recompute()
rows = groupStats(component, top=6)

How it works. project(kind, "person") links two people when they share a neighbour: here, a phone or an address (claims belong to one person each). After removing people who share nothing, each connected component is a group bound by shared identifiers. groupStats(field) profiles every group: size, internal and boundary edges, density, and conductance (how sealed off it is). The largest groups here have 10, 8, 7 and 6 members. Three of those four are the planted rings, and the other is an ordinary extended family at one address. Structure finds the candidates, but it can’t tell a ring from a family.

4.2 Score the groups Intermediate

You want to rank groups by how suspicious they are, using what the network doesn’t show directly: the claims behind them.

makeFromGDF("claims-SIMULATED.gdf")
addNodeField("claims", INTEGER, 0)
addNodeField("doctors", VARCHAR, None)
addNodeField("lawyers", VARCHAR, None)
addNodeField("days", VARCHAR, None)
for p in (kind == "person"):
    cs = [c for c in p.getNeighbors() if c.kind == "claim"]
    p.claims = len(cs)
    p.doctors = ";".join(sorted(set(d.label for c in cs for d in c.getNeighbors() if d.kind == "doctor")))
    p.lawyers = ";".join(sorted(set(d.label for c in cs for d in c.getNeighbors() if d.kind == "lawyer")))
    p.days = ";".join(str(c.day) for c in cs)
project(kind, "person")
removeDisconnected()
recompute()
def profile(members):
    docs = set(d for p in members for d in (p.doctors or "").split(";") if d)
    laws = set(d for p in members for d in (p.lawyers or "").split(";") if d)
    days = [int(d) for p in members for d in (p.days or "").split(";") if d]
    span = max(days) - min(days) if days else 0
    n = sum(p.claims for p in members)
    score = n / len(members) * (2 if len(docs) == 1 else 1) * (2 if len(laws) == 1 else 1) * (2 if span <= 31 else 1)
    return n, len(docs), len(laws), span, score
for rw in groupStats(component, show=False)[:6]:
    print(rw["size"], profile(rw["members"]))

How it works. Before projecting, each person is given fields summarising their claims: how many, which doctors, which lawyers, which days. The projection copies the fields along. Then each group is profiled, and the profile becomes a simple score: claims per member, doubled if the group uses a single doctor, a single lawyer, or files everything within a month. The three rings stand out at once. Each files 17 to 34 claims within 25 days through one doctor and one lawyer. The family has 5 claims over 216 days, through four doctors.

Pitfalls. A score is a way to order the review queue, not a verdict. Every ingredient here has an innocent explanation (families do share doctors), and real schemes adapt to known rules. Prefer scores whose parts an investigator can read and challenge over a black box.

5 Money flows

5.1 Follow the big money round in circles Intermediate

You want round trips: money that leaves an account and comes back through others, which disguises where it came from.

Ingredients: transfers-SIMULATED.gdf · subgraph · findCycles

makeFromGDF("transfers-SIMULATED.gdf")
len(findCycles(6))
subgraph(amount > 10000, "big money")
loops = findCycles(8)
for c in loops: print(c.annotation)

How it works. findCycles(maxLength) lists the directed cycles of up to maxLength accounts. In a busy network many cycles occur by chance: there are 155 short ones among the 400 accounts. subgraph(amount > 10000) keeps only large transfers and the accounts they join (20 accounts, 19 transfers). In that graph the only cycles left are the four planted round trips, of 3, 4, 5 and 6 accounts. Each loop’s amounts shrink a little at every hop (a fee skimmed each time), and the hops run on consecutive days. Both are checks to apply to any cycle you find.

Variations. showTime(day, field) with a time window on the edges, or colorBy(day, ...), shows whether a loop’s transfers follow each other in time, as a real round trip must.

5.2 Find the mule Intermediate

You want accounts that collect many small payments and pass the money on in a few large ones.

makeFromGDF("transfers-SIMULATED.gdf")
addNodeField("small_in", INTEGER, 0)
for n in g.nodes:
    n.small_in = sum(1 for e in n.getInEdges() if 800 <= e.amount < 1000)
for n in sortBy(small_in)[-3:]: print(n.name, n.small_in, n.indegree, n.outdegree)
mule = sortBy(small_in)[-1]
showOnly(egoNetwork(mule))
mule.color = red

How it works. A money mule’s signature is fan-in, fan-out: many senders, few recipients, the money passing straight through. Deposits just under a reporting threshold are structuring. This recipe counts, for each account, the incoming transfers between 800 and 1,000. Most accounts have none or one or two. One stands out with 26 such deposits from 26 different senders, and three large transfers out. egoNetwork shows its neighbours.

6 Case study

6.1 Case study: from claims to a referral Intermediate

An investigation is a sequence of narrowing steps, each one reviewable. Here is the whole path for the claims data in one place:

makeFromGDF("claims-SIMULATED.gdf")
def phones(p): return set(x for x in p.getNeighbors() if x.kind == "phone")
pairs = findDuplicates(label, among=(kind == "person"), threshold=0.7, show=False)
mergeNodes([(a, b, sc) for a, b, sc in pairs if phones(a) & phones(b)])
ss("resolved")
addNodeField("claims", INTEGER, 0)
for p in (kind == "person"): p.claims = sum(1 for c in p.getNeighbors() if c.kind == "claim")
project(kind, "person")
removeDisconnected()
recompute()
suspects = [rw for rw in groupStats(component, show=False) if sum(p.claims for p in rw["members"]) >= 2.5 * rw["size"]]
[(rw["size"], sum(p.claims for p in rw["members"])) for rw in suspects]

Resolve duplicates (with a saved state to return to). Give each person their claim count, link people by shared identifiers, and keep only groups filing at least 2.5 claims per member. Exactly the three rings remain (the smallest files 17 claims among 6 people). For a referral, export each group’s subgraph from the resolved network (use("main"), subgraph(...), exportPDF) with its profile. The steps above, saved with history(), are the method section of the report.

7 Capstone

7.1 Capstone: an investigator’s workbench Advanced

The problem. A claims investigator works through a review queue. For each suspicious group they want to see the group in context, read its profile, drill into one person, and flag or clear them. This recipe builds that workbench inside GUESS:

  • a ranked queue of groups, with a slider for the minimum score;
  • buttons to step through the queue;
  • a profile that updates for each group;
  • a click on a person shows their claims;
  • right-click items to flag a person or expand their neighbourhood;
  • a legend and a status bar showing progress.
makeFromGDF("claims-SIMULATED.gdf")
addNodeField("claims", INTEGER, 0)
addNodeField("flag", VARCHAR, None)
for p in (kind == "person"): p.claims = sum(1 for c in p.getNeighbors() if c.kind == "claim")
project(kind, "person")
_ = removeDisconnected()
recompute()
forceAtlas2Layout()
queue = sorted(groupStats(component, show=False), key=lambda rw: -sum(p.claims for p in rw["members"]) / rw["size"])
state = {"i": 0, "min": 2.0}

def score(rw): return sum(p.claims for p in rw["members"]) / rw["size"]
def in_queue(): return [rw for rw in queue if score(rw) >= state["min"]]

def show(i):
    q = in_queue()
    if not q:
        info.text = "no group above the threshold"
        return
    state["i"] = i % len(q)
    rw = q[state["i"]]
    g.nodes.color = "210,210,210"
    g.nodes.size = 6
    g.nodes.labelvisible = false
    rw["members"].color = orange
    rw["members"].size = 16
    rw["members"].labelvisible = true
    info.text = (f"group {state['i'] + 1}/{len(q)}: {rw['size']} people, "
                 f"{sum(p.claims for p in rw['members'])} claims, score {score(rw):.1f}")
    StatusBar.setStatus(f"{len(flag == 'suspect')} flagged so far")

def threshold(v):
    state["min"] = float(v)
    show(0)

def details(n):
    person.text = f"{n.label}: {n.claims} claims; flag: {n.flag or 'none'}"

def flag_it(n):
    n.flag = "suspect"
    n.color = red
    details(n)

slider = Slider(0, 5, 2, step=0.5, label="minimum claims per member", onChange=threshold)
info, person = Label(""), Label("click a person for details")
panel = Panel("Review queue", slider, Button("Previous", onClick=lambda: show(state["i"] - 1)),
              Button("Next", onClick=lambda: show(state["i"] + 1)), info, person, position="bottom")
ui.dock(panel)
graphevents.clickNode = details
item = NodeEditorPopup.addItem("Flag as suspect")
item.menuEvent = flag_it
item2 = NodeEditorPopup.addItem("Show neighbourhood")
item2.menuEvent = lambda n: showOnly(egoNetwork(n, 2))
show(0)
center()

How it works.

  • The queue. The work happens in the person network linked by shared identifiers (the shared-identifiers recipe), with each person’s claim count copied along. queue holds every group, ranked by claims per member. state keeps the reviewer’s place and the slider’s threshold, and in_queue() is the queue above that threshold.
  • The controls. Slider, Button and Label build the controls, and Panel and ui.dock put them below the graph. Moving the slider calls threshold. The buttons call show, which greys out everyone else, enlarges and labels the group, and rewrites the profile line.
  • Clicks and menus. graphevents.clickNode = details runs on every click on a node. NodeEditorPopup.addItem adds the two right-click menu items, and StatusBar.setStatus keeps the flag count in view.

Variations.

  • exportApp() saves the workbench as a page that a colleague can open with the same data and controls.
  • Add a Dropdown of doctors to review by provider, or a second slider over day with showTime to replay a ring’s activity.
  • A Checkbox that hides cleared groups.
